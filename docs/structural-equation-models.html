Borrelia Competence of Peromyscus

Structural equation models

Structural equation models are a special case of path analysis, which describe the directed dependence among sets of variables, often viewed as directed acyclic graphs (e.g., Figure 19). These are an ideal tool for understanding the relationships within and among many levels of complex system with many variables. We will use this approach to model Borrelia infection competence of Peromyscus.

Figure 19: Example directed acyclic graph, with two response (Y) variables and two predictor (X) variables. In this case, Y1 is both a resonse (X1, X2) and a predictor (Y2).

Modeling approach

Towards finding a good explanatory model of Borrelia infection competence among Peromyscus, we will fit a few different SEMs with different objectives. The key data for each approach will be Peromyscus data for eight NEON sites. The variables we will consider in each model are

  1. Study design variables including NEON site and plot identity (siteID, plotID), and year of the observations;

  2. Mouse general characteristics including individual identity (iid), sex (sex_male), reproductive status (sex_mature), and weight (weight);

  3. Mouse behavioral characteristics including capture time (cap_prop_night), trapability (weighted_trapability), trap diversity (weighted_trap_diversity), and movement (avg_move_dist);

  4. Interactions with ticks specifically whether or not ticks were attactched at capture (ticks_attached, nymphalTicksAttached, larvalTicksAttached).

  5. Immunity information including PCA for gene expression of resistance and tolerance genes (expr_PC1, expr_PC2) as well as Borrelia infection status and burden (Bb_infected, Bb_burden); and

  6. Climatic information including principal components for short-term weather (wthr_PC1, wthr_PC2) and long-term average site climate (clim_PC, clim_PC2).

Complete cases

Burden

Figure 20 visualizes a SEM fit to a “complete” data set (i.e., only observations for which all variables were measured). As such, it has some limitations in its power, but it is the most “defensible” explanatory model because it does not draw conclusions beyond the scope of its data. Models using more of the data will be explored in the next section.

While this model is very interesting, one of the primary paths of interest is not significant (behavior -> ticks -> resistance -> burden -> tolerance). It is possible that the “behavior -> ticks” path is improved by a model which is not limited by the completeness restriction. Table 11 summarizes the fit (\(R^2\)) of each of the component models.
